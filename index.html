Robots that adapt.

I study how robots learn efficiently, adapt to new situations, and follow human intent through language.

More about my work

My work connects perception, imitation learning and real robots. I’m interested in how they understand long, multi-step tasks: what matters now, what comes next, and when to change course.

I work with Professor Danica Kragic at KTH. My PhD with Professor Robert Mahony at ANU focused on learning-based visual control.

Attention for manipulation

Attention from action alone

Seeker CoRL 2026

Seeker learns where to look through action prediction alone, without spatial labels. The same attention guides zoom, background augmentation and 3D point filtering.

3×
real-robot success

60% versus 20% for the strongest baseline, averaged across lighting and background changes.

From attention to visual robustness

RoboSaGA CoRL 2024

Learning to ignore background and lighting changes.

A complementary use of attention: policy saliency guides background augmentation so behaviour cloning is more robust to visual changes.

Task-aware efficiency

A shallow CNN is enough —
when it knows where to look

FocusPool CoRL 2026

FocusPool selects shallow visual features according to the task’s progress. Knowing where to look makes a small encoder enough for effective control.

1.5%
of ResNet-18’s
encoder parameters

Using only the shallow L1 features.

The L2 model (5.8%) reaches comparable RVT-2 performance with roughly half the demonstrations: 50 versus 100 in the simulation comparison.

Skill transfer & spatial generalisation

Reusing skills in new settings

PALM RA-L 2026

Settings can look different overall, yet look similar near the gripper. PALM pairs this local visual similarity with proprioceptive representation alignment to reuse manipulation skills across workspace positions, camera viewpoints and robot embodiments.

System integration

Real-world robot systems

I integrate hardware and software through ROS, connecting perception, sensor synchronisation, calibration and real-time control in complete robot systems.

Human–robot collaboration

GoferBot

I co-developed GoferBot, a vision-based human–robot interaction system for collaborative furniture assembly. It combines human action recognition, visual servoing and handover behaviour so the robot can coordinate naturally with a person.

Watch GoferBotPaper

Warehouse picking · ACRV team

Cartman

I contributed as a member of the ACRV team that built Cartman, combining custom hardware with robotic perception for warehouse picking. The system won the 2017 Amazon Robotics Challenge, and the team leader later spun this work out into Lyro Robotics.
